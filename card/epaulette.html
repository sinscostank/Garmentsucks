The epaulette began as a rifle strap

Before rank, it stopped the sling sliding off the shoulder.

probable

French army epaulettes appear around 1763 as officer marking, but their origin is a shoulder belt that held a rifle sling or ammunition belt in place. Over a century the functional ribbon widened and gilded into a badge of rank. The general lineage is well covered; the exact date is conventional rather than precise. On a field jacket the epaulette is a short strap across the shoulder, buttoned at the neck end — and the jungle jacket dropped it on the third pattern because it snagged in brush.
