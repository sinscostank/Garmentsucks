A field guide to the American tropical combat uniform, the jackets that led to it, and what the fashion after it did with them. History first, then construction, then the clothes on a body.

Start here

The archive follows one path: history → war issue → fabric → construction → details → closures → how-to → designers → brands. The feed below is ordered that way.

Designers are the author-led houses — Maison Margiela, Rei Kawakubo, Yohji Yamamoto, Junya Watanabe, Mihara Yasuhiro, Raf Simons, Helmut Lang, Undercover and Issey Miyake — where the garment is an argument, not a product line. Brands are the commercial and workwear labels that borrow the same vocabulary.

War issue is the section of garments actually issued and worn: the M-1941, N-1 and tanker jackets, the M-51 fishtail parka, the sailor's bell-bottoms, and the HBT, OG-107, M-65 and jungle fatigue trousers — photographed as original issue or as period documentary photographs, never reproduction.

Confidence labels. established primary or institutional evidence plus corroboration · probable strong but single-source or inferential · unresolved sources conflict, or the story is legend. A label applies to the entry as a whole, not to every sentence in it.

Scope: the American tropical combat uniform (1963–67), the jackets before and after it, and the fashion that reworked them. Not yet a general history of garments.

A personal archive for learning the fashion industry

This is not a magazine and not a shop. It is one person's research notes, made public: a place to learn how clothes are actually made, why they look the way they do, and where the language of style comes from.

The path. Start with history — why a pocket is slanted, why a button hides behind a flap, why a cloth was chosen for a climate. Move to construction — seams, cloth, hardware, measurements. Then to styling: how a garment is worn, what a silhouette says, and what people mean when they call something "archive". The long-term plan is a guided route from "what is this jacket?" to "why does it matter, and how do I wear it?"

The model. The house this archive studies most is Maison Martin Margiela: anonymous, numbered, and built on the idea that the clothes should speak instead of the designer. That is the ambition here — no personality, no trend list, just evidence and explanation.

Method. Every entry states a confidence level: established (primary or institutional evidence plus corroboration), probable (strong but single-source or inferential), unresolved (sources conflict, or the story is legend). Sources are listed on every page, and no claim is upgraded for narrative neatness.

Glossary. Bellows pocket — a pocket with a side pleat that expands. Placket — the reinforced strip carrying the buttons. Bartack — a dense bar of stitches at a stress point. Action back — pleats across the back for arm movement. Gusset — an inserted panel that adds room. Yoke — the shoulder panel on a shirt or jacket. Felling — a seam whose raw edges are folded inside and stitched. Hand — how a cloth feels. IR — infrared; a dye chosen to behave differently there.
